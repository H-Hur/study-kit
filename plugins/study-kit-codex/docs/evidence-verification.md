# Verify claims and distinguish derived results

Use for source checking, factual revision, language editing, and textbook audit.
Updated 2026-10-09 from issue #7.

Distinguish the original page/PDF, extracted text, and a generated summary. Tool output
may be any of these; do not assume every fetch is a model summary or that a quoted
passage in a summary is faithful. Treat a summary as a discovery aid. For numbers,
dates, equations, institutional attribution, and other factual claims, inspect the
original passage and context. Check ambiguous extraction against the page image or
original file. Record the source, page/section, relevant conditions, and a preserved
original where available. If it cannot be checked, mark the claim unverified rather
than promote a plausible summary or quotation to evidence.

Checking a source's first page verifies bibliographic identity, not claims elsewhere
in it. When rewriting a factual sentence, re-verify every claim in that sentence,
including retained claims, against the relevant original passages. Preserve units,
assumptions, uncertainty, and qualifying language. Existing verified source locations
can be reused if they still support the new sentence; a fresh broad search is not
required for every grammatical change.

Separate **sourced facts** from **derived results**. A calculation may prove a result
under stated assumptions, check dimensions, reproduce a reported number, test a
figure, or detect transcription mistakes. It cannot independently establish that an
empirical input or reported measurement is true. Cite the basis for those inputs;
label your calculation as a derivation or consistency check and state its assumptions.
Keep useful computational checks, but report precisely what they verify. Never turn
agreement with a model into a claim that a source has established an empirical fact.

When tools or sources disagree, resolve the discrepancy against the original and its
version/context. If unresolved, preserve the uncertainty in the revision queue and
publication checks; do not choose a value merely because it appears more often.
